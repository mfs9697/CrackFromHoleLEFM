\author{}
\date{Pilot manuscript}

\begin{abstract}
The incremental direction of a hole-initiated crack is examined in an
asymmetric elastic plate using linear elastic fracture mechanics and the
maximum tangential stress criterion. A stress-selected point on the circular
hole defines the initiation location and a frozen material-side normal.
Only the first crack segment is prescribed along that normal. Subsequent
directions follow from the current-tip stress-intensity factors after one
elastic solution of each fixed, complete crack geometry. A constant 4-mm
increment produces an accepted history from 4 to 92 mm. The opening-mode
intensity increases throughout this history, whereas positive mode mixity
reaches a maximum at 68 mm and changes sign between the solved 84- and
88-mm states. This change reverses the sign of the incremental turn while
the absolute crack direction remains negatively inclined. Reflection-paired
tip meshes, prescribed-field controls, strict residual checks, and
crack-opening-displacement comparisons support the numerical interpretation.
The late redirection is consistent with increasing free-boundary influence
rather than unloading, although this single trajectory does not isolate
boundary effects from accumulated curvature. The next geometry is qualified
but physically unsolved. The findings establish a verified fixed-increment
pilot sequence, not increment-size convergence or a prediction of arrest,
branching, or boundary intersection.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} crack trajectory; circular hole; linear elastic
fracture mechanics; maximum tangential stress; mode mixity; interaction
integral; crack-opening displacement.

\section{Introduction}
Stress concentrations around holes identify possible crack-initiation
sites, but an initiation site alone does not determine the subsequent
trajectory. Once a finite crack exists, its direction depends on the
mixed-mode field at its current tip and on the entire existing crack
geometry. A direction-selection calculation must therefore update the
elastic problem as the crack advances, rather than assign the complete
trajectory at initiation.
% TODO: verified literature on crack initiation and trajectories from holes/notches.

The present pilot considers a single crack growing from a circular hole in
a finite, asymmetric plate. The scientific question is how its direction
evolves when each new segment is determined only by the current-tip
opening- and sliding-mode stress-intensity factors. The maximum tangential
stress (MTS) rule supplies a local turn; accumulating that turn updates the
absolute direction in a fixed initiation frame. The imposed finite
increment specifies the geometric advance, while the stress-intensity
factors determine its direction.

The central result is a change in the sign of mode II while mode I continues
to increase. Positive mode mixity first grows, reaches a maximum, and then
decreases through zero. The resulting change in turning sense occurs as
the tip approaches the right free boundary. The calculation provides
evidence for this evolution within one qualified discretization; it does
not by itself establish a causal boundary-effect decomposition or
convergence of the predicted trajectory. Numerical verification is used to
separate this physical interpretation from extraction artifacts and from
the eventual linear-solver limit.

\section{Problem formulation}
\subsection{Geometry, material, and loading}
The plate occupies $[0,A]\times[-B,B]$, with $A=\PlateAMM$ mm and
$B=\PlateBMM$ mm. A circular hole of radius $R=\HoleRMM$ mm is centered at
$(\HoleCxMM,\HoleCyMM)$ mm. The finite-element hole boundary is represented
by a 480-segment polygon with the fitted initiation point retained. The
material is isotropic, with $E=210000$ MPa and $\nu=0.30$, under plane
strain. For the crack geometry $\Gamma_k$ ending at $P_k$, the elastic
problem is
\begin{align}
 \nabla\!\cdot\bm{\sigma}&=\bm0 &&\text{in }\Omega_k,\label{eq:equilibrium}\\
 \bm{\varepsilon}&=\tfrac12(\nabla\bm u+\nabla\bm u^{\mathsf T}),\qquad
 \varepsilon_{33}=0,\label{eq:strain}\\
 \bm{\sigma}&=2\mu\bm{\varepsilon}
       +\lambda\,\operatorname{tr}(\bm{\varepsilon})\bm I,
 \quad \mu=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)},\quad
 \lambda=\frac{E\nu}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}.\label{eq:constitutive}
\end{align}
The top and bottom tractions are $(0,\sigma_0)$ and $(0,-\sigma_0)$,
respectively, with $\sigma_0=1$ MPa. The vertical sides, hole boundary, and
crack faces are traction free. Rigid-body motions are removed by imposing
$u_x=u_y=0$ at the lower left corner and $u_y=0$ at the lower right corner.
There are no body forces.

All reported SIFs correspond to this unit traction and have units
MPa$\sqrt{\mathrm m}$. For a fixed geometry, positive scaling of the
traction scales both SIFs equally and preserves their ratio and the MTS
direction. The sequence studied here is a direction-selection calculation
under normalized loading, not a load-versus-time fracture-growth history.

\subsection{Stress-selected initiation and the frozen frame}
The uncracked-hole elastic field selects the initiation point from the
maximum positive tangential boundary stress. In the recorded initiation
procedure, T6 stresses sampled at three material-side offsets are
extrapolated linearly to the boundary, followed by a local quadratic
angular fit. With tensile threshold $\sigma_c=300$ MPa, the normalized
initiation rule is
\begin{equation}
 \phi_* = \mathop{\operatorname{arg\,max}}_\phi
      \sigma_{tt}^{+}(\phi;\sigma_0),\qquad
 \lambda_{\mathrm{ini}}=
      \frac{\sigma_c}{\max_\phi\sigma_{tt}^{+}(\phi;\sigma_0)},
 \quad \sigma_{tt}^{+}=\max(\sigma_{tt},0).\label{eq:initiation}
\end{equation}
The accepted fitted angle is $\phi_*=\PhiStar^\circ$. The initiation point is
\begin{equation}
 P_0=(\MouthX,\MouthY)\ \mathrm m.
\end{equation}
The fitted peak stress is
$\PeakStress$ MPa at unit loading, giving $\lambda_{\mathrm{ini}}=\LoadFactor$.
This initiation condition supplies the location; it is not reused as a
growth or arrest criterion along the crack.

The frozen basis is
\begin{equation}
 \bm n_{\mathrm{mat}}=\begin{bmatrix}\NormalX\\\NormalY\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad \bm t=\begin{bmatrix}-n_{\mathrm{mat},y}\\n_{\mathrm{mat},x}\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:frozenframe}
\end{equation}
The normal points radially out of the hole and into the material; $\bm t$
is its counterclockwise rotation. All absolute crack angles $\theta_k$
below are measured relative to this frozen normal, not to the global
$x$ axis. The first segment is prescribed by
\begin{equation}
 \Delta a=4\ \mathrm{mm},\qquad \theta_1=0,\qquad
 P_1=P_0+\Delta a\,\bm n_{\mathrm{mat}}.\label{eq:first}
\end{equation}
An earlier first-angle sweep was an independent verification exercise and
does not determine $\theta_1$ in this formulation. The study retains one
active crack; subsequent competing initiation sites are not modeled.

\section{Incremental crack-path formulation}
\subsection{Current-tip frame and stress-intensity factors}
At $P_k$, the local forward direction is the \emph{last} segment direction,
$\bm e_1^{(k)}=(P_k-P_{k-1})/\Delta a$, with
$\bm e_2^{(k)}=(-e_{1y}^{(k)},e_{1x}^{(k)})$. This frame is not the
mouth-to-tip chord. With $r$ measured ahead of the tip along $x_1$, the
signed conventions correspond to
\begin{equation}
 K_I^{(k)}=\lim_{r\to0^+}\sqrt{2\pi r}\,\sigma_{22}(r,0),\qquad
 K_{II}^{(k)}=\lim_{r\to0^+}\sqrt{2\pi r}\,\sigma_{12}(r,0).
 \label{eq:sifdefinition}
\end{equation}
These are evaluated numerically by the interaction integral described
below. The complete previous crack geometry is retained during each
elastic solution.

\subsection{The implemented MTS rule}
Let $\alpha$ be a turn relative to $\bm e_1^{(k)}$, and write
$c=\cos(\alpha/2)$ and $s=\sin(\alpha/2)$. The implemented angular
coefficients are exactly
\begin{align}
 S(\alpha)&=K_I^{(k)}c^3-3K_{II}^{(k)}s c^2,\label{eq:stt}\\
 T(\alpha)&=K_I^{(k)}s c^2+K_{II}^{(k)}c(1-3s^2).
 \label{eq:trt}
\end{align}
Their physical stress amplitudes carry the common factor
$1/\sqrt{2\pi r}$. Since $S'=-3T/2$, the direction rule finds a tensile
stationary direction by solving $T(\alpha)=0$. In the implementation the
root lies in $[-80^\circ,80^\circ]$: a bracket of $\pm10^\circ$ around a
clipped initial estimate $-2K_{II}/K_I$ is used first and expanded to the
full interval if necessary. For $|K_I|<10^{-12}$ the initial estimate is
$\operatorname{sign}(K_{II})70.5^\circ$ instead. The routine sets $\alpha=0$ when
$|K_{II}|<10^{-12}$ in the stored SIF units, and includes an opposite-angle
safeguard if the computed circumferential coefficient is compressive.
The accepted history has $K_I>0$ and small tensile-branch turns. No angular
scan is performed during propagation.

For small mode mixity, expansion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:trt} gives
$\alpha\simeq-2K_{II}/K_I$ in radians. This relation explains the turning
sign but does not replace the root calculation used for the results.

\subsection{Finite-increment recurrence}
Define the direction vector in the frozen frame by
\begin{equation}
 \bm e(\theta)=\cos\theta\,\bm n_{\mathrm{mat}}
                 +\sin\theta\,\bm t.\label{eq:direction}
\end{equation}
The incremental and absolute angles are distinguished in the recurrence
\begin{align}
 \Delta\theta_{k+1}&=\alpha_{\mathrm{MTS}}
                     (K_I^{(k)},K_{II}^{(k)}),\label{eq:turn}\\
 \theta_{k+1}&=\theta_k+\Delta\theta_{k+1},\label{eq:angleupdate}\\
 P_{k+1}&=P_k+\Delta a\,\bm e(\theta_{k+1}).\label{eq:pointupdate}
\end{align}
Thus each fixed current geometry is qualified, solved once, and used to
append one segment. Only the first direction and the increment length are
prescribed. The accepted first-tip SIFs are
$K_I^{(1)}=0.366479612185$ and $K_{II}^{(1)}=4.19826648316\times10^{-6}$,
which give $\Delta\theta_2=\theta_2=-0.001312722142^\circ$.
This previously qualified first-tip state seeds the long run; the accepted
subsequent elastic states are $P_2$ through $P_{23}$.

\section{Numerical implementation and verification}
\subsection{Geometry and paired tip discretization}
The crack is represented by its complete polyline with distinct upper and
lower face topology, including previous kink vertices. A temporary
appended-hole carrier supports geometric identification; its crack faces
are collapsed to the zero-width polyline while preserving separate face
identities. A structured core is aligned with the last segment and paired
under local reflection across that segment. The asymmetric physical
exterior is retained through conforming graded triangulation. T3
triangles are converted to straight-sided quadratic T6 elements.

The fixed production scales are
\begin{equation}
 \frac{h_{\mathrm{tip}}}{\Delta a}=0.00675308135,\quad
 \frac{r_i}{\Delta a}=0.10,\quad
 \frac{r_o}{\Delta a}=0.65,\quad
 \frac{r_c}{\Delta a}=0.75.\label{eq:meshscales}
\end{equation}
For this 4-mm increment these give $h_{\mathrm{tip}}=0.0270123$ mm,
$r_i=0.4$ mm, $r_o=2.6$ mm, and $r_c=3$ mm. The grading transition is
4 mm and the far-field size cap is 2.5 mm. The paired core contains 12678
T3 triangles. All primary FE-nodal integration support lies inside this
core, with a fixed fingerprint of 11316 participating elements.

Geometry and mesh checks retain the physical boundary vertices, distinct
crack faces, shared intact ligament, conforming seams, positive element
areas and T6 Jacobians. The mesh-angle and adjacent longest-edge-ratio
gates are $20^\circ$ and 1.8, respectively. These checks establish
discretization integrity, not mesh-independent physical predictions.

\subsection{Interaction/domain integral}
For actual field $(\bm\sigma,\bm\varepsilon,\bm u)$ and a unit auxiliary
mode $m$, the implemented local-frame density gives
\begin{equation}
 I^{(m)}=\int_{\Omega_k}
 \left[-(\bm\sigma^{(m)}\!:\bm\varepsilon)\delta_{1j}
 +\sigma_{ij}u^{(m)}_{i,1}
 +\sigma^{(m)}_{ij}u_{i,1}\right]q_{,j}\,\dd A,
 \qquad i,j\in\{1,2\}.\label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
Actual gradients use the T6 displacement field. Analytical near-tip
auxiliary stresses and centered finite-difference derivatives of auxiliary
displacements are used, with the recorded derivative step
$h=\max(10^{-7}\ \mathrm m,10^{-5}r)$. A radial nodal weight is one for
$r\leq r_i$, decreases linearly to zero at $r_o$, and is zero outside.
Its T6 interpolant supplies the FE-consistent gradient in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:interaction}. Integration uses the 16-point triangular rule.
For auxiliary amplitude $K_{\mathrm{aux}}=1$ and plane strain,
\begin{equation}
 K_m=\frac{E'}{2K_{\mathrm{aux}}}I^{(m)},\qquad
 E'=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2},\qquad m\in\{I,II\}.\label{eq:normalization}
\end{equation}
The normalization and sign convention are those used in the recorded
extraction procedure.

\subsection{Independent COD postprocessing}
Native upper/lower T6 face nodes are paired in the last-segment frame.
If $[\![u_i]\!]$ denotes the upper-minus-lower displacement jump behind
the tip at distance $r$, the apparent COD SIFs are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{bmatrix}K_I^{\mathrm{app}}(r)\\K_{II}^{\mathrm{app}}(r)\end{bmatrix}
 =\frac{\mu}{\kappa+1}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{r}}
 \begin{bmatrix}{[\![u_2]\!]}\\{[\![u_1]\!]}\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad \kappa=3-4\nu.\label{eq:cod}
\end{equation}
Linear and quadratic fits in $r/\Delta a$ extrapolate these quantities to
$r=0$. The four fitting windows are $[0.04,0.20]$, $[0.04,0.30]$,
$[0.08,0.30]$, and $[0.12,0.30]$ times $\Delta a$, with 38, 55, 44,
and 34 native samples. COD verifies a second extraction route from the
\emph{same} elastic field. The production direction always uses the EDI
SIFs; window/order scatter is not a statistical uncertainty interval.

\subsection{Solver and evidence checks}
The unclamped symmetric stiffness is reduced to free degrees of freedom,
symmetrized within the audited formulation, and reordered by a symmetric
approximate minimum-degree permutation. Parameter-free symmetric
Gauss--Seidel preconditioning is used with conjugate gradients. The
reported-residual tolerance is $10^{-10}$, the iteration limit is 5000,
and the independently computed true-relative-residual gate is
$5\times10^{-10}$. Constrained degrees of freedom remain exactly zero.
Only an accepted elastic field is checkpointed, before SIF/COD/MTS
postprocessing.

Prescribed nodal pure-I, pure-II, and tiny-mixed $(K_I,K_{II})=(1,10^{-4})$
fields test modal recovery and leakage on each qualified mesh. Recovery
and tiny-mixed relative-error gates are $2\times10^{-4}$; the pure-mode
cross-leakage and superposition gates are $10^{-10}$. The observed maximum
tiny-mixed relative error is $\SyntheticRelativeError$. These controls
qualify extraction capability and topology rather than the global physical
finite-element error.

A short regression reproduces a previously accepted two-segment $P_2$
state before extending the path. Historical straight 8-mm calculations
provided separate extraction and controlled-mesh verification; they are
not points on the propagated trajectory and do not establish convergence
of the present 4-mm path.

The authoritative long-run archive contains 22 accepted compact physical
records ($P_2$--$P_{23}$) and 23 qualification records
($P_2$--$P_{24}$). A semantic audit against the atomic path state confirms
the accepted SIFs, mode mixity, turns, absolute directions, and geometry.
The maximum direction-record difference is $4.36\times10^{-14}$ degrees;
the other compared scientific quantities agree exactly. The maximum
accepted true residual is $\MaxTrueResidual$, and iteration counts range
from 2442 to 3011. Supporting diagnostics are given in Fig.~\ref{fig:quality}.

\subsection{COD comparison and extraction sensitivity}
All eight stored COD fit definitions retain positive mode mixity at $P_{21}$
and negative mode mixity at $P_{22}$, corroborating the bracketed sign change
without making COD the direction selector. Differences remain nonzero and
depend on the fitting definition (Fig.~\ref{fig:cod}). Over all 176 fits,
the largest absolute COD--EDI turning difference is $\MaxCODError^\circ$,
at $P_{23}$ for a linear fit over $[0.12,0.30]\Delta a$.
The quadratic-fit turns at $P_{23}$ range from $\CODQuadMin^\circ$ to
$\CODQuadMax^\circ$, compared with the EDI value $0.624347038^\circ$.

Near local symmetry, relative differences can be large despite small
absolute errors. At $P_{21}$ the same rear-window linear fit gives mode
mixity about $\CODNearZeroPercent$\% above EDI, while its turning-angle
difference is only $\CODNearZeroTurnDiff^\circ$. Thus visual overlap of
full-history curves is not sufficient to establish extraction accuracy.
The late fitting sensitivity is a postprocessing limitation to investigate,
not an error bar on the physical path. Both routes also share any error in
the underlying elastic solution.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Interpretation of late redirection}
The accepted sequence shows reorientation under increasing $K_I$.
The change in turning sign is directly associated with the change in
$K_{II}/K_I$, as required by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trt}--\eqref{eq:angleupdate}.
The shrinking right-side ligament makes increased free-boundary influence
a consistent physical interpretation. However, the boundary distance and
the accumulated crack geometry change together. A control with a more
distant boundary, or matched fixed-geometry calculations, is needed to
isolate that influence. The result supports a mode-mixity explanation of
the computed redirection; it does not prove that the boundary is its sole
cause.

\subsection{Discretization and boundary proximity}
The directions are selected from the field before adding a finite straight
segment. A 4-mm step therefore samples a continuously changing boundary-value
problem at discrete geometries. No increment-size study is available for
this path. The interpolated zero in Eq.~\eqref{eq:zeroestimate} should be
read against the 4-mm spacing of its bracketing states, not as a highly
resolved physical crack length. A smaller-increment family and independent
mesh/EDI-domain checks at the late tips are needed to assess path uncertainty.

Local reflection pairing and synthetic recovery suppress known extraction
leakage mechanisms, but do not bound the physical discretization error.
Similarly, residual and element-quality gates measure numerical acceptance,
not fracture-mechanics convergence. The historical 8-mm verification cannot
be transferred into a mesh-independence claim for this evolving polyline.

\subsection{Last accepted state and model scope}
The geometry ending at $P_{24}$, with total polyline length 96 mm, passes
geometric and prescribed-field qualification. It has no accepted physical
record. The reported attempted PCG solution did not satisfy the strict
acceptance criterion; the available compact archive does not retain a
failure trace that would quantify its stagnation. The physical history
therefore ends at $P_{23}$, without retrospectively relaxing the gate.
This is a numerical limitation, not evidence of fracture arrest or boundary
intersection.

The model is quasi-static and linearly elastic. Crack advances are imposed
for direction selection; no fracture-toughness growth law, dynamic
instability, branching rule, plastic-zone assessment, or subsequent
multicrack initiation calculation is provided. These restrictions define
the scope of the present trajectory rather than mechanisms excluded by
the results.

\section{Conclusions}
A stress-selected circular-hole point and one prescribed normal segment
provide the initial state for an incremental trajectory whose later
directions are determined by current-tip EDI SIFs and the implemented MTS
rule. The accepted fixed-increment history reaches 92 mm at $P_{23}$.
Positive mode mixity peaks at $P_{17}$, crosses zero between $P_{21}$ and
$P_{22}$, and becomes negative while opening-mode intensity continues to
increase. This changes the sign of the incremental turn and reduces the
negative absolute inclination late in the history.

The sign reversal is shared by all stored COD fitting definitions, although
their late-path differences show finite extraction sensitivity. Paired-core,
synthetic, solver, and archive checks establish a reproducible numerical
sequence within the chosen discretization. The qualified unsolved extension
must remain separate from the physical results. Increment-size convergence,
late-tip mesh/domain checks, and a controlled boundary comparison are the
next requirements for stronger predictive and causal claims.
