Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I would like to submit the manuscript entitled **“Incremental LEFM Crack-Path Prediction from an Off-Center Circular Hole: Mode-Mixity Reversal and Numerical Sensitivity”** for consideration as an original research article in *Archive of Applied Mechanics*.

The paper addresses the prediction and numerical assessment of a crack trajectory emanating from an initially uncracked circular hole in an elastically loaded plate. The initiation location is selected from the uncracked-hole stress field; after a prescribed outward-normal first segment, each successive crack direction is computed from the maximum tangential stress criterion using the mixed-mode stress-intensity factors of the complete current crack geometry.

The principal contribution is a carefully qualified computational benchmark, rather than a new fracture criterion. The study combines interaction-integral extraction with crack-opening-displacement comparisons, a centered-hole symmetry control, and independently propagated paths under changes in structured-tip and exterior-mesh resolution. It identifies a positive maximum followed by a sign reversal in the signed mode-mixity ratio, with a corresponding reversal of incremental turning sense. Separately, a coupled 4-, 2-, and 1-mm refinement family exhibits contracting geometric differences and a nearly stable increment-normalized turning response. The paper explicitly distinguishes these observations from a formal convergence proof, an isolated crack-increment study, or a crack-growth/arrest prediction.

I believe this combination of a geometry-updating LEFM formulation, numerical qualification, and explicit limits on the conclusions is relevant to the journal's readership in fracture and computational mechanics. The resulting benchmark can also support future assessments of cohesive and time-dependent fracture models without implying that such models are part of the submitted work.
