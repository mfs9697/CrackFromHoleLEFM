# Scientific review of the SIF asymmetry audit

Read-only review, 4 October 2026. Repository: `mfs9697/CrackFromHoleLEFM`; branch: `sif-asymmetric-mesh-audit`; audited commit: `4b1eaf33f53177ec9f9126daabdad0d20539f121`.

## 1. Executive conclusion

**The symmetric benchmark has identified a compelling numerical mechanism; the physically asymmetric Mode-II value remains a credible candidate, not a validated physical result.**

The unpaired symmetric meshes produce artificial Mode II through two distinguishable routes: the computed FEM field loses reflection parity, and the EDI evaluation of a prescribed, nodally sampled pure-I singular field develops cross-mode leakage. Exact reflection pairing suppresses both manifestations to floating-point scale: actual-field COD in Step57, actual-field EDI in Step58, and prescribed exact-nodal EDI in Step59. The coherent explanation is restoration of discrete reflection invariance and cancellation, rather than elimination of every approximation error.

For the asymmetric 8-mm crack, Steps33–38 provide substantive evidence: genuinely nested annular refinement stabilizes EDI; halving the measured tip scale changes its reference ratio by only about 0.387%; native COD remains positive and moves closer to EDI. These findings support a weak, positive mixed-mode response on the **fixed discretized geometry**. They do not establish the continuum circular-hole value, a quantified uncertainty interval, or an accurately resolved nonzero kink.

The most efficient next program is to preserve the asymmetric physical problem and test a **locally reflection-paired discretization**, beginning with a geometry/topology feasibility assessment and then a mesh-only candidate. A solve becomes worthwhile only after the candidate preserves the problem, matches achieved resolution, and passes prescribed-field extraction controls. Local mesh pairing must never become a displacement-symmetry constraint.

**Evidence scope.** I inspected the branch's audit documentation, relevant MATLAB drivers, production extraction, mesh conversion/collapse, refinement, and solver boundary-condition/loading code. I used read-only GitHub access and a separate text snapshot; no repository file, branch, PR, mesh, or FEM solution was changed or created. The branch does not contain the local Step34/38/45/47/56–59 MAT checkpoints or their compact result artifacts. Thus numerical values below are investigator-reported results in committed documentation, checked for arithmetic and consistency with the drivers—not independently reproduced numerical outputs.

Dedicated Step42/43 drivers, result files, and documents are absent from the audited tree; branch-history inspection also found no dedicated implementation under those names. Their available evidence is the retrospective Steps39–43 summary in `STEP44.md`. I therefore cannot verify separate Step42/43 synthetic-control claims. This gap should be resolved from the investigator's original records before a publication-level provenance claim.

## 3. Mechanism inferred from the symmetric benchmark

For reflection across the local crack axis, define P acting on displacement by P u(x1,x2)=[u1(x1,-x2), -u2(x1,-x2)]. Pure Mode I belongs to the P-even sector; pure Mode II to the P-odd sector. Rigid displacement gauges should be removed consistently when testing these relations.

In an exactly paired mesh, the local interpolation spaces, material integration and—when the whole problem is symmetric—assembled stiffness/loading respect this reflection. A symmetric equilibrium solution can therefore remain in its pure-I sector to floating-point accuracy. The Mode-II interaction density for that field cancels between reflected integration points. An unpaired discretization need not preserve either the field parity or that cancellation.

There are two related effects:

1. **Solved-field contamination:** unequal reflected element shapes/connectivity give different discrete approximations on opposite sides. A physically symmetric loading can generate small numerical tangential face jumps and P-odd field components. COD extrapolation can amplify their radial variation, explaining why Step47 intercepts are much larger and less stable than its EDI residual.
2. **Extraction of an interpolated prescribed field:** even exact pure-I nodal values do not imply an exactly pure-I interpolated gradient on unmatched upper/lower triangles. Quadratic interpolation of the square-root displacement field produces geometry-dependent gradient errors; the signed weighted integral of those errors can change sign with remeshing.

Steps45c/51 bypass the second effect and dramatically reduce leakage. Steps57/58 suppress the first effect and restore the actual-field cancellation. Step59 closes the prescribed-field side of the chain. Their combination is much stronger than sign co-variation alone. It does not separate percentages attributable to assembly, equilibrium error, displacement interpolation, q interpolation, or finite-window fitting in the earlier actual solution.

Several source-code details sharpen this interpretation:

- `T3toT6_fast.m` creates every midside at the arithmetic midpoint and identifies edges by **node IDs**. It preserves duplicated crack faces and shares conforming intact edges, but supplies no quarter-point singular basis. Equal node coordinates alone are insufficient; complete connectivity must pair.
- `collapse_pencil_faces_to_midline.m` moves face coordinates while retaining connectivity and separate face IDs. It can yield coincident face sampling without reflected neighboring triangles. Thus matching crack-face grids does not imply a symmetric FEM approximation.
- `native_COD_audit.m` classifies faces and midsides topologically, rotates displacement into the crack frame, and forms apparent KI/KII from normal/tangential jumps multiplied by the square-root radial scale. It **does call PCHIP** for the opposite face; matched grids make this effectively nodal evaluation, but the routine is not intrinsically interpolation-free. Polynomial intercepts are produced by the drivers, not by this shared routine. The COD frame uses mouth-to-tip direction, whereas EDI uses the last crack segment; these agree for this straight-crack audit.
- `SIF_LEFM_interaction_EDI.m` uses T6 actual displacement gradients and an FE-interpolated radial q when requested. The audited drivers explicitly select `fe_nodal` and 16 GP; routine defaults remain legacy analytic-radial q and 7 GP. Results from those defaults cannot be silently combined with the later audit.
- The “direct analytical Gauss” override calls the **same** `local_aux_LEFM_fields` helper used for auxiliaries. Stresses are analytical, but displacement derivatives are centered finite differences with h=max(1e-7,1e-5 r) times a scale factor. It bypasses nodal interpolation; it is neither an independent analytical implementation nor an exact closed-form derivative test.
- For FE-nodal q, participating Gauss points are selected by nonzero interpolated q gradient, not strict membership in the nominal annulus. Elements straddling either radius can contribute outside it. Any local patch proposal must cover this actual element support.

Ordinary quadratic elements must approximate the singular field; quarter-point constructions can explicitly represent the elastic square-root singularity. This supports a separate future basis test, but does not make it the best first intervention after the pairing evidence. [Official Abaqus contour-integral documentation](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-contintegral.htm).

**Why Step59 is decisive but limited:** KI still differs from unity by 3.9147e-5 while KII is at roundoff. Pairing cancels the odd leakage without removing even-sector approximation error. Likewise Step57's COD KI values remain window-dependent. A symmetry-exact zero can coexist with appreciable error in other quantities.

## 4. What the symmetric benchmark does and does not say about asymmetric KII

The benchmark establishes that the implementation can preserve zero Mode II when geometry, discrete topology and numerical operations respect the relevant reflection. It also demonstrates a mechanism capable of generating false signals on unpaired meshes. This justifies testing balance in the actual problem.

It provides **no transferable numerical floor, correction, percentage uncertainty, or validation certificate**. The asymmetric crack has different global geometry, crack length, achieved scales and higher-order fields. Its genuine solution does not possess the benchmark's reflection parity. In particular, subtracting Step58/59 roundoff values would have no physical or numerical rationale.

The asymmetric result deserves more confidence than the early near-zero calculations. Its positive sign persists through real nested refinement and across both extraction routes; EDI stabilizes strongly; COD changes in the expected direction after tip refinement. Step44's very small same-mesh leading-field leakage further disfavors a basic extraction normalization or leading-pure-I leakage of that measured magnitude as the entire explanation of Step38.

However, “positive candidate of order 1e-4” is presently stronger than “validated physical ratio 1.0795940665e-4.” Missing evidence includes:

- sensitivity to deliberate local balance in the unchanged asymmetric problem;
- stability of the genuine asymmetric component under a discriminating subsequent resolution or topology/transition control;
- a defensible reconciliation of persistent COD/EDI differences and higher-order finite-window effects;
- retained-hole polygon and collapse/mouth regularization convergence after numerical balance is controlled;
- uncertainty in the Stage-I selected mouth/normal if the claim concerns the selected physical initiation problem, rather than a prescribed fixed mouth;
- publication-grade recovery of the original checkpoints, full settings and numerical provenance.

The 0.387% refinement change, 9.4–20.1% COD discrepancy, and symmetric residual magnitudes each describe a different diagnostic. None is currently a percentage error bound on the physical asymmetric KII.

## 5. Competing hypotheses still consistent with the evidence

| Hypothesis | Current assessment | Discriminating evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| A real weak finite-length mixed-mode response dominates Step38 | **Strongly supported as a fixed-geometry candidate**, unresolved quantitatively. Positive EDI/COD and nested stability favor it. | Persistence on a locally paired discretization, followed by geometry and residual numerical checks. |
| Mesh-induced contamination materially shifts the real signal | **Plausible and important.** The symmetric mechanism exists, but its magnitude on Step38 is unknown. | Matched achieved resolution with deliberate pairing on the original asymmetric domain. |
| COD extrapolation / ordinary-T6 immediate-tip bias explains most remaining method disagreement | **Strongly supported as a contributor**, not uniquely established. | Matched physical sampling and validated higher-order field fits; later isolated tip-basis or resolution change if needed. |
| Retained-hole polygon, collapse near the mouth, or initiation-frame bias contributes at the tiny-signal scale | **Unresolved.** Earlier NArc studies were nonmonotone and remeshed; mouth shifts produced clear KII responses. | A later isolated geometry or prescribed-mouth study on a stabilized discretization. |
| Residual equilibrium, weighting, auxiliary-derivative or quadrature errors interact with higher-order actual fields | **Possible secondary contributors.** Direct-Gauss controls make a large universal leading-field integration bias less likely. | Same-field diagnostics targeted to the observed failure; no generic sweep. |
| The entire Step38 signal is artificial | **Not excluded**, but less consistent with the positive independent COD evidence and refinement behavior than in the early audit. | Collapse/sign instability under controlled balance would strengthen this hypothesis; stable signal under independent controls would weaken it. |

Valid higher-order Williams terms can bias finite-window COD extrapolation. They do not, by themselves, violate exact interaction-integral path independence in a correctly posed homogeneous straight-crack continuum problem; any such EDI sensitivity reflects discretization, implementation or applicability conditions. The standard interaction construction separates modes using auxiliary fields. [Official interaction-integral theory](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAETHERefMap/simathe-c-stressintfact.htm).

## 6. Recommended next research program

Use theory → one small step → verification → interpretation → next step.

**Stage A: evidence and feasibility, no solve.** Inspect the existing Step38 checkpoint's exact collapsed boundary and crack topology, locate the full q-gradient element support, and determine whether a reflection-paired disk and a conforming transition can fit without touching the hole/mouth or protected exterior. Recover the missing Step42/43 records if readily available. Freeze settings and baseline results. This is the single next action recommended below.

**Stage B: one mesh-only candidate and prescribed-field qualification.** Build one locally paired patch from one triangulation and its reflected connectivity. Preserve the accepted asymmetric geometry and exterior. Match measured tip and annular resolution rather than nominal mesher parameters. Check pure-I, pure-II and tiny-mixed prescribed fields before spending a physical solve; use a direct-Gauss check only if prescribed nodal recovery fails unexpectedly.

**Stage C: one actual asymmetric solve, conditional on qualification.** Solve the entire asymmetric domain on that exact candidate with unchanged loading, material and anchors. Compare predeclared COD and fixed-annulus EDI with Step38. Interpret mesh sensitivity; do not select whichever result resembles the old value.

**Stage D: choose one follow-up from the outcome.** If balance changes the signal materially, investigate transition/exterior coupling before refinement. If it preserves a weak positive signal but COD remains fit-sensitive, validate a higher-order displacement fit on stored fields. If numerical diagnostics stabilize, isolate retained-hole geometry or mouth regularization next. A precise kink/root calculation comes only after these tests resolve the signed SIF to the accuracy the claim requires.

Alternatives ranked by expected information per cost:

| Rank | Alternative / control | Information and cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Existing-data provenance, geometry and q-support audit | Cheapest; establishes whether the proposed experiment can preserve the problem and what it actually isolates. |
| 2 | One scaled, higher-order Williams displacement fit on existing Step34/38 fields, with manufactured higher-order controls | No solve; helps distinguish COD extrapolation from a stable singular coefficient. Include rigid motions, appropriate regular terms and conditioning diagnostics; do not impose pure-I parity or tune terms to match EDI. Requires some new postprocessing implementation later. |
| 3 | Local paired mesh plus prescribed unit/mixed controls | No physical solve; directly qualifies the intervention implicated by Steps57–59. More informative now than further symmetric radius sweeps. |
| 4 | One whole-domain asymmetric solve on the qualified local patch | Highest direct information about transfer of the symmetric mechanism, but appreciably greater cost. |
| 5 | One controlled geometry/mouth-regularization change after numerical stabilization | Necessary for continuum interpretation; premature while topology and field/extraction ambiguity remain. |
| 6 | Quarter-point/enriched-tip implementation or independent solver | Valuable independence, especially if the gap persists; larger implementation/verification cost and new confounders. |
| 7 | More uncontrolled PDE remeshing, broad h/radius/order sweeps, or another globally symmetric solve | Low current gain. More nodes and another symmetric zero do not answer the asymmetric question. |

A globally reflected counterpart of the asymmetric problem could test KII sign reversal, but two independently solved mirrored cases can inherit mirrored numerical errors and still pass. It is a useful convention/covariance test, not a substitute for convergence. A paired-patch submodel driven by interpolated old interface displacements may cheaply isolate local representation effects, but inherits baseline boundary-data error and cannot validate the full-domain result.

## 7. First proposed experiment in enough detail to implement later

**Question:** At approximately the Step38 achieved resolution, does deliberately pairing the crack-tip and extraction-region mesh materially change the tiny signed response of the unchanged asymmetric problem?

**Physics held fixed.** Retain the full plate, off-center hole at [0.17,-0.02] m, exact saved retained-hole boundary, prescribed mouth [0.19998887220,-0.020817033905] m, crack direction/endpoints and a0=8 mm, original collapse/mouth construction, E, nu, plane-strain/stress flag, unit loading, traction boundaries and the original full-domain minimal anchors. Do not rerun Stage I, replace the hole polygon by a newly generated one, import the centered half-domain constraints, reflect the global domain, or constrain paired node displacements. Work from the **saved collapsed Step38 mesh**, since reconstructed geometry need not reproduce its final boundary near the mouth.

**Patch extent.** Use the crack-local frame and construct a reflection-invariant disk or polygonal disk with the existing slit. Pair the complete T3/T6 tip fan, face sampling through the COD windows, and every element carrying q-gradient support for the primary EDI domain. Prefer covering all three existing domains if geometry and transition quality allow it. Pairing only a few tip triangles is an incomplete test of the demonstrated EDI mechanism.

The primary absolute annulus remains [0.8,5.2] mm; secondary existing outer radii are 4.0 and 6.4 mm. A paired boundary around 7 mm is a **feasibility proposal**, not an approved dimension: a0=8 mm does not by itself prove adequate clearance from the actual hole polygon, nor adequate transition width. Determine clearance and element-support envelopes from the saved geometry. If all-domain coverage leaves too narrow a collar, prioritize the 5.2-mm domain; label the 6.4-mm domain explicitly as crossing the transition. Do not quietly change historical radii to obtain a favorable result.

**Construction and transition.** Retain an outer cavity contour made of existing baseline edges and preserve every triangle outside it, up to bookkeeping renumbering. Within it, create a symmetric inner interface and pair the patch by reflecting one upper triangulation, reversing reflected orientation. Share nodes across the intact ligament ahead of the tip; retain distinct coincident T3/T6 crack-face IDs behind it, sharing only the tip. Retriangulate only the collar between the paired interface and the fixed asymmetric cavity boundary. Use conforming shared interface edges/midsides, graded sizes and no artificial free seam, duplicate intact interface or hanging node. The collar may be asymmetric; record it as a changed region. If a nominal circular boundary would cut existing triangles, enlarge the cavity to an edge-aligned boundary and bound the resulting change before accepting it.

**Matched quantities.** Record tip-adjacent edge distribution and fan geometry, annular edge median/p90 and directional distributions, quality/minimum angle, native COD radial lists and populations, element support of q, transition dimensions, total nodes/elements, and exact unchanged exterior/boundary segments. Match Step38 hTip≈0.05402465 mm and annular achieved scales as closely as practicable. Suggested design gates are tip median within 5%, annular median/p90 within 10%, and T6 growth at most 30%; these are proposed design tolerances, not demonstrated error bounds. If pairing requires larger changes, explicitly reclassify the study as a combined balance/resolution intervention. Preserve native face radii where practical; otherwise qualify synthetic recovery at the changed radii and report the sampling difference.

**Predeclared extraction.** Preserve conventional midpoint T6, solver/quadrature settings and the same COD/EDI implementation during this intervention. COD primary comparisons use Step38's original [0.04,0.20], [0.04,0.30], [0.08,0.30] a0 windows and degrees 1/2. Add [0.12,0.30] only as a named secondary comparison with the later controls. Report both KI and KII intercepts, raw apparent profiles, sample counts, minimum r/hTip, face mismatch, fit conditioning and residuals. Fit only actual native positions; report matched-position comparisons separately. Missing/ill-conditioned fits are failures to evaluate, not zero results.

EDI primary uses fixed [0.8,5.2] mm, FE-nodal q, 16 GP and **actual solved U**. Secondary historical domains use fixed [0.8,4.0] and [0.8,6.4] mm if interpretation remains useful; no automatic h-dependent inner radius. Record individual SIFs, signed ratio, cross-domain spread and support relative to the transition. Displacement symmetry is not an acceptance condition for this asymmetric solution: its antisymmetric content can be physical. Same-point field differences or parity decomposition are explanatory diagnostics only.

**Prescribed controls before a solve.** On the candidate, test exact pure-I leakage first and, if it passes, pure-II normalization and the tiny-mixed response via linearity or an explicitly identified direct replay. Include COD branch/face checks and an affine interpolation check. For a fully paired q-support region, pure-I→II cancellation should approach floating-point scale even in the asymmetric exterior, since the extraction is local. If it does not, inspect the support, pairing, q and frame before solving. Pure-II and mixed recovery must also pass: a scheme that merely suppresses all Mode II is unacceptable.

**Provenance.** Save the exact candidate and its verified map to the baseline, then solve that candidate without regeneration. Record content hashes of mesh/U, source revision and full numerical settings in any future cache. Existing caches typically use checkpoint path/size/timestamp plus selected method/domain fields; those are practical reuse guards, not complete source/content provenance. Step47 reconstructed a candidate from summary metrics, whereas Step57 reused saved connectivity; adopt the stronger Step57 practice.

## 8. Explicit stopping/decision criteria

The following numbers are proposed prospective research gates. They are not inferred uncertainty bounds.

| Gate | Proceed only if / decision |
|---|---|
| Geometry feasibility | The saved physical boundaries and crack can remain exact, a paired q-support region fits, and a conforming quality-preserving collar is possible. If not, stop before meshing and redesign the bounded cavity or primary coverage. |
| Mesh-only qualification | Complete element/midside pairing in the declared region; correct slit and intact topology; positive T3 areas and T6 Jacobians; no gaps/overlap/bridges; exact exterior preservation; adequate native windows. Suggested local minimum angle ≥20° and no worse quality tail than baseline; stop on violations. |
| Matched scales/resources | Achieved scales meet the declared 5%/10% matching targets and growth ≤30%, or the changed interpretation is agreed before the solve. More than one uncontrolled parameter shift invalidates a clean balance comparison. |
| Prescribed pure-I extraction | With full paired support, aim for absolute leakage ratio ≤1e-10. A failure indicates an incomplete cancellation test; investigate without a physical solve. Do not force a physical U to meet this threshold. |
| Prescribed unit/mixed extraction | Unit-II response has correct sign and normalization; full recovery-matrix error should be no worse than Step44's measured approximately 4.91e-8 Frobenius error if the candidate is intended as an improved extraction platform. If this target proves unattainable at matched scales, diagnose before changing it. Tiny-mixed recovery must resolve the input without calibration subtraction. |
| One physical solve | Only after the preceding gates, solve the exact candidate once; save mesh/U immediately. Verify finite U, free-DOF residual and loading/reactions with existing solver quantities before extraction. Set algebraic tolerances prospectively according to solver precision; do not equate a small residual with spatial accuracy. |
| First comparison | A persistent positive ratio near 1e-4, materially stable KI and improved/consistent COD behavior supports robustness to this intervention. Proposed screening scale: |delta ratio|≤1e-5 and relative |delta KI|≤1e-3. Passing is one robustness result, not validation. |
| Material change | Ratio collapse, sign reversal, change exceeding the predeclared 1e-5 screen, or new strong domain/fit sensitivity means the old value is mesh-sensitive. Stop and investigate transition/exterior propagation or resolution confounding before another solve. A changed value is informative, not automatically an experimental failure. |
| Success followed by one targeted test | If the physical signal survives and diagnostics improve, choose one test of paired-patch extent/transition, paired resolution, or geometry according to the remaining largest ambiguity. Do not launch all three. |

For COD, success means that changes across predeclared matched windows/degrees become understandable and stable, preferably narrowing the unexplained EDI gap. Selecting one agreeable fit is not success. For EDI, small cross-domain spread is necessary evidence of consistency but cannot replace mesh and geometry checks. If the first candidate already satisfies its preflight purpose, stop mesh tuning: optimize information, not the smallest residual.

Before any **additional** FEM solve, require a written competing-hypothesis decision: what unresolved explanation it tests, what result would distinguish it, and why existing field/postprocessing or mesh-only work cannot answer it. When those cheaper controls answer the question, stop without another solve.
